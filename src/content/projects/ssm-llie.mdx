## The problem

State-space (Mamba-style) restorers model long-range structure efficiently, which suits low-light images with uneven lighting, noise and colour shifts. Illumination priors should help them too, but how the prior enters the network matters. In early probes, injecting illumination signals directly into the state-space core gave no stable gain and sometimes produced unstable or visually unsafe output.

## The idea

The final design keeps the illumination guidance away from the core dynamics. A small estimator predicts an illumination map from the dark input, the map is turned into prompt tokens, and a residual sidecar adapter injects them at the restorer's bottleneck. The state-space blocks themselves are left untouched, so the guidance can shape the result without destabilizing the backbone. I developed this illumination-aware conditioning strategy and ran the evaluation on the benchmark datasets.

## Results

Over three training seeds on LOL-v1, the Prompt-Adapter raised PSNR from 25.01 dB to 25.95 dB and lowered LPIPS from 0.2292 to 0.2273 compared with the same restorer without it. A control that feeds a learned but input-independent prompt through the same sidecar scores about as well, so the evidence credits the sidecar route itself. Showing that the illumination content adds more on top of it is still open.

## Why it matters

A stronger prior is not automatically a better one if the route it takes destabilizes the model. Treating conditioning as a question of where guidance should enter, rather than how much of it to add, gives a lightweight way to use illumination cues in efficient state-space restorers without redesigning the backbone.
