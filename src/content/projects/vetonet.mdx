## The problem

Low-light enhancement can be deterministic, producing a single fixed output for each input, or generative, using a model such as a diffusion model to synthesize missing detail. Generation can recover more, but some of what it adds is invented: where the sensor captured little, a weak but real signal and a plausible hallucination are easy to confuse. The question is when a low-light pipeline should generate at all.

## The idea

VetoNet decides region by region whether to keep the deterministic result or to use a generative one, depending on whether generation helps there. The decision is calibrated with conformal risk control, a statistical method that keeps the risk of harm from generation below a chosen level without assuming a particular data distribution.

## Why it matters

Hallucination is often treated as the unavoidable price of generative restoration. VetoNet treats it instead as a risk to be measured and bounded, with the stated aim of making that control auditable. Generation is then used only where its measured benefit justifies its risk, and the deterministic result keeps its reliable structure everywhere else.
