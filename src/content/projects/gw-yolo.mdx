## The problem

Gravitational-wave detectors record strain, a measure of how a passing wave stretches and squeezes their arms. Astrophysical signals in that record, such as the chirps from merging pairs of black holes or of neutron stars, are often faint. Glitches, transient noise artifacts that arise inside a single detector, can look much like those chirps, and at low signal-to-noise ratio a search that examines each detector on its own tends to raise false alarms.

## The idea

Coherent GW-YOLO converts strain data into Q-transform time–frequency images, where a chirp traces a rising track that a YOLO-based object detector can find and box. Candidates from the LIGO Hanford and Livingston detectors are then compared on timing, frequency evolution, and candidate consistency, because a gravitational wave reaches both detectors while a glitch stays local to one.

## Why it matters

Detection that supports early warning has to be reliable, not only sensitive. Building cross-detector coincidence into the framework itself, from labels through evaluation, targets false positives in the low signal-to-noise regime, where faint signals and glitches are hardest to tell apart. The low-light work in [VetoNet](/projects/vetonet/) rests on the same principle: claim only what the measurements support.
