## The problem

Enhancing a low-light image takes more than raising its brightness. A good result has to correct exposure and keep fine texture and object boundaries intact while still looking natural. These aims pull in different directions, so a method has to balance them.

## The idea

PRIN builds on the Retinex model of image formation, splitting a dark image into reflectance, the intrinsic content of the scene, and illumination, the light that falls on it. Each component then gets its own job: wavelet-domain refinement recovers texture and boundaries in the reflectance and adjusts brightness smoothly and coherently in the illumination.

## Why it matters

Recovering fine detail and correcting global brightness are different problems, and the Retinex prior grounds the split between them in how an image physically forms. The goal is enhancement that preserves what is structurally meaningful in a scene, not only what becomes visible.
